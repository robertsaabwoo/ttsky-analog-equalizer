# Verification methodology

How this receiver was checked, in the order the questions had to be answered.
The deep chronological logs are `xschem/tuning/NOTES.md` (CDR, §1-§20) and
`xschem/tuning/ctle/NOTES_CTLE.md` (CTLE and end to end, §C1-§C26); this page
is the map, not a duplicate of them.

Everything below is ngspice on sky130 models. There is no silicon yet, and no
layout — so there is no post-extraction simulation. That is stated plainly
rather than glossed over.

---

## 1. The question each stage had to answer

| # | question | how it was answered | deck | result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is the channel? | The channel is not a cable — it is the Tiny Tapeout analog pin path, spec'd at < 500 Ω series and < 5 pF pad. Modelled at the specified *bound*, so the design target is the worst case the shuttle can legally hand us. | the TT analog spec, re-modelled at its *bound* (§C15) | 63.7 MHz pole, −13.7 dB at the 300 MHz Nyquist, eye fully closed at the pad (§C16) |
| 2 | Does the equalizer invert it? | AC first (cheap), across a 45-point sizing ladder × 3 candidate channels in a single ngspice run. | `xschem/tuning/ctle/ladder_tt.spice` | the zero has to sit far lower than the previous tuning assumed (§C17) |
| 3 | Does AC gain translate into an *eye*? | It does not, automatically: the ripple-optimal sizing scored 0.295 UI while the eye-optimal one scored 0.430 UI. Ranked by eye directly instead. | `xschem/tuning/ctle/eye_tt5.spice` | +5× eye height, +4.8× timing margin over the old sizing (§C17) |
| 4 | Does it hold over PVT? | 27 corners: tt/ss/ff × −40/27/125 °C × 1.62/1.8/1.98 V, AC, parsed by a script with explicit pass criteria rather than by eye. | `xschem/tuning/ctle/ac_corner_tt.spice` → `xschem/tuning/ctle/cornerreport.py` | combined channel+CTLE stays within ±2 dB of flat at Nyquist across the whole box (§C18) |
| 5 | Does the *eye* hold over PVT? | AC in dB does not map linearly onto an opening in mV, and group delay moves the sampling phase — so the two corner extremes were re-run as real PRBS7 eye diagrams. | `xschem/tuning/ctle/eye_pvtc_worst.spice`, `xschem/tuning/ctle/eye_pvtc_best.spice` | worst corner still 174 mV / 0.350 UI; output common mode moves 362 mV over the box (§C22) |
| 6 | Does the CDR lock at all? | Full-loop transient, after a frequency-plan error was found and fixed (§14) and the loop filter was resized ~10× (§16). | `xschem/tuning/CDR_tune_tb.sch` | locks at 600.63-600.78 MHz, jitter 0.68 % UI RMS (§16b, §17e) |
| 7 | Does it start reliably? | Cold-start was polarity-dependent: ~50 % of power-ups never acquired. A startup precharge cell was designed, then swept over 45 corners driven by a real supply ramp with no `.ic` anywhere. | `xschem/tuning/pvt/` tier T1 | 45/45 pass; seed 0.693-0.908 V, release 136-167 ns (§20a) |
| 8 | Can the oscillator even reach the rate over PVT? | Open-loop tuning-range sweep, 11 corners, with an explicit pass criterion. | `xschem/tuning/pvt/` tier T0 | 6 pass / 5 fail — 125 °C and 1.62 V are out of reach. Documented, accepted, not fixed (§20b) |
| 9 | Do the two blocks work as one? | The merge gate. CTLE and CDR simulated as a single loop through the pad RC for the first time. | `xschem/tuning/e2e_ctle_cdr_tb.spice` | locks at 600.64 MHz at the same control voltage as the CDR alone — so it is a real lock, not a coincidence (§C23) |
| 10 | Does it lock on *real* data? | 0101 is the easiest possible pattern for a bang-bang detector. PRBS7 (max run 7 UI) generated by `xschem/tuning/ctle/gen_prbs.py`, run to 3 µs. | `xschem/tuning/e2e_c25b_prbs.spice` | settles: +37 mV, −26 mV, −2.7 mV — a damped ring, ~3× the 0101 acquisition time (§C25-B) |
| 11 | Does polarity or amplitude break it? | Two more 3 µs runs: data inverted, and source amplitude halved. | `xschem/tuning/e2e_c25c_pol.spice`, `xschem/tuning/e2e_c25d_sens.spice` | polarity-independent; 200 mVpp comfortable, 100 mVpp marginal (§C25-C, §C25-D) |
| 12 | *Why* is the residual dither what it is? | A mechanism was proposed from the netlist (charge-pump mismatch) and then **measured** instead of assumed. | `xschem/tuning/cp_mismatch.spice` | hypothesis refuted by 80×; the real cause is ordinary bang-bang quantisation, which predicts 35 mV pp against 33.7 mV measured (§C25-E) |
| 13 | How much jitter? | Recovered clock dumped and analysed by a stdlib edge parser. | `xschem/tuning/e2e_c26_jitter.spice` → `xschem/tuning/jitter_parse.py` | cycle-to-cycle 1.48 % UI RMS — usable. The phase-wander number from the same run is **documented as not usable** and the correct measurement is specified but unrun (§C26) |
| 14 | Does the drawn design match the validated one? | The sandbox blocks were promoted into the real schematics and then compared at **netlist** level, not by eye. | `xschem/tuning/e2e_blocks.inc` vs a fresh netlist | all 17 leaf subcircuits matched exactly, identical top-level instance list (`xschem/STATUS.md`) |
| 15 | Is the layout flow real? | `make lvs` wired end to end against `src/project.v` before drawing anything. | `mag/Makefile` | runs correctly; reports 0 devices on the layout side against 224 on the source side, which is the right answer for an empty frame (`mag/LAYOUT_HANDOFF.md`) |

## 2. How the PVT harness works

`xschem/tuning/pvt/` is a three-tier harness rather than a single monolithic
sweep, because the tiers cost wildly different amounts of compute:

- **T0** — open-loop VCO tuning range. Cheap. Answers "can the oscillator reach
  the baud rate here at all?" before anything expensive is attempted.
- **T1** — the startup precharge cell alone, from a real supply ramp. Cheap,
  45 corners.
- **T2** — full-loop acquisition at corners. ~2 h; generated and ready, never
  run.

The pattern is: **generate decks from the schematics** (`gen_pvt.py`), **run
them strictly sequentially under a resource guard** (`run_pvt.sh` →
`safe_ngspice.sh`), **collect with explicit pass criteria in code**
(`collect_pvt.py`), so a corner passes or fails by a written rule and not by
someone looking at a waveform. `collect_pvt.py` even distinguishes "VCO tuning
range exhausted" from "precharge failure", so an expected failure cannot be
mistaken for a new one.

One sky130 detail worth knowing: **MOS corners and R/C corners are independent
axes**. `.lib ss` moves only transistors; `.lib hh`/`ll` move only resistors and
capacitors. The stock `.lib` sections cannot be combined, so the harness emits
the underlying `.include` lines directly.

## 3. The compute constraint, and why it shows up in the code

Development happens on a 7.8 GB / 4-core VM that an unguarded ngspice has
already OOM-crash-rebooted once, losing work. Every simulation therefore runs
through `xschem/tuning/safe_ngspice.sh`, which applies a hard `ulimit -v`, a
wall-clock `timeout`, a `/proc/meminfo` watchdog and `nice`. Sweeps are done
*inside* one ngspice process with `alter`/`alterparam` loops rather than by
launching N processes, and vectors are always `save`d explicitly — a bare
`write foo.raw` stores every node at every timepoint, which is precisely what
caused the crash.

A full end-to-end 3 µs transient takes 400-1200 s of wall time. `run_c25.sh`
therefore batches the four end-to-end runs and appends each result to
`xschem/tuning/c25_summary.txt` as it finishes, so a killed session loses at
most one run.

## 4. Measurement traps this project actually hit

These are in the logs because each one produced a *wrong number that looked
right*. They are the reason the results table cites sections rather than
asserting values.

- **`meas ... RISE=<n>` measures wherever the n-th edge happens to be**, not at
  the time you meant. Frequency was first measured at 1.00 µs — mid-acquisition
  — while every other measurement used a 1.8-1.9 µs window (§C24).
- **A 60-cycle averaging window cannot resolve a 2 MHz offset** when 79 mV of
  control-voltage ripple is worth ±14 MHz of instantaneous frequency. One
  "frequency error" was noise in its own measurement (§C25-C).
- **`reset` discards `alter` but keeps `alterparam`.** A corner loop silently
  produced identical numbers for every corner, with no error (§C22).
- **`.op` treats a capacitor as an open**, so any power-on-reset circuit is dead
  on arrival in a deck that starts from an operating point (NOTES §19).
- **A ring oscillator started from `uic` never oscillates** — all-nodes-at-zero
  is exactly its metastable DC point (`CLAUDE.md` §3).
- **`let clkp = v(clk+)` silently produces nothing**: ngspice parses the `+` as
  an operator and the vector simply never appears in the raw file, with no
  error (`CLAUDE.md` §3).
- **xschem resolves a missing symbol silently**, emitting a truncated netlist
  rather than failing. The `test/` suite exists partly to catch that class of
  breakage without needing xschem at all.

## 5. What is *not* verified

Stated explicitly, because a results table that only lists successes is not a
verification report.

- **No layout, therefore no DRC-clean GDS, no extracted parasitics, and no
  post-layout simulation.** Every number in this repository is schematic-level.
- **No PVT on the combined CTLE+CDR loop.** The end-to-end runs are all at
  tt / 27 °C / 1.8 V. Block-level PVT exists for the CTLE (27 corners), the
  precharge (45) and the VCO (11).
- **No trustworthy sampling-phase jitter figure.** Cycle-to-cycle jitter is
  measured and fine; the phase error *relative to the data eye* — the number
  that actually decides whether the link works — needs a ≥ 5 µs run measured
  against the ideal bit grid after the loop has stopped moving. §C26 says so in
  its own words rather than quoting the bad number.
- **No monte-carlo mismatch analysis** on the phase detector latches, which
  have only ~5 % CML/inverter margin (§12a).
- **The 125 °C and 1.62 V corners fail** on VCO reach, and are accepted rather
  than fixed (§20b).
